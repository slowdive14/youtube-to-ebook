# Pressure Points: Inside the Eleventh-Hour Scramble Reshaping American Power and Global Politics

With just days remaining before the midterm elections, the machinery of American democracy is experiencing an unprecedented stress test. From federal grand jury subpoenas landing on the desks of local election workers in California to federal court filings challenging ballot dynamics in the remote expanses of Alaska, the Trump administration has embarked on an aggressive, eleventh-hour intervention campaign. 

Yet the domestic political churn is only one facet of a broader atmosphere of institutional turbulence. Across the Potomac, the Pentagon is undergoing a leak hunt marked by mandatory polygraph tests and the abrupt dismissal of seasoned combat leaders. Meanwhile, halfway across the globe in Jerusalem, an inconclusive national vote threatens to plunge Israel back into legislative paralysis. Together, these divergent developments illustrate a world grappling with fractured norms, institutional distrust, and high-stakes maneuvering.

---

## Subpoenas and Ballot Stacking: The Election-Eve Offensive

In the final stretch of the midterm cycle, federal involvement in state-run elections has intensified sharply, focusing primarily on two contrasting battlegrounds: Los Angeles County and the state of Alaska.

In California, administration officials have zeroed in on roughly 20,000 registered voters, asserting without public documentation that they are likely non-citizens whose ballots should be sequestered. Non-citizen voting has repeatedly been proven by independent academic and government reviews to be statistically vanishingly rare, and a federal judge summarily dismissed the administration’s emergency request to halt the tallying of those votes. 

The battle, however, did not stop in the courtroom. Justice Department officials signaled that local administrators who refuse federal directives to set aside specific ballots could face criminal investigations. The threat materialized into reality when the Department of Justice served federal grand jury subpoenas to local election workers, including at least two senior officials within the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder’s office. The registrar publicly denounced the move as an unsubtle attempt at voter intimidation.

The aggressive federal push mirrors similar initiatives that collapsed upon closer inspection. Just weeks earlier in Nevada, state officials audited a list of approximately 200 allegedly unlawful voters provided by the Department of Homeland Security. Every single individual on the DHS list turned out to be an eligible, lawful American citizen.

Simultaneously, in Alaska, the Justice Department has plunged into a very different election dispute: the opaque tradition of "ballot stacking." Following investigative reporting revealing that operatives tied to Democratic challenger Mary Peltola recruited a third-party candidate sharing the exact legal name of incumbent Republican Senator Dan Sullivan, federal prosecutors issued dozens of subpoenas across the state. 

While the tactic of running duplicate-name candidates or propping up minor contenders to dilute an opponent’s coalition is an age-old—and generally legal—political dark art (one that Alaska Republicans themselves utilized earlier this cycle to divide the Democratic primary vote), the administration has mobilized the Justice Department to challenge the outcome. Backed by the White House, federal attorneys have filed a lawsuit attempting to block Alaska from certifying the election if Senator Sullivan loses by a narrow margin. Legal scholars have noted that such a preemptive challenge faces steep constitutional hurdles, as states retain primary authority over election administration.

## Lie Detectors and Sidelined Brass: A Reckoning at the Pentagon

Inside the Department of Defense, a quiet crisis of confidence has erupted into public view. Following a series of unauthorized media disclosures revealing that U.S. munitions stockpiles have been severely depleted by sustained military actions involving Iran, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth launched an internal crackdown.

"Access to confidential and secret information is a sacred trust," Hegseth warned. "And those who betray that trust will be met with the full force of the law."

The hunt for leakers led to measures virtually unprecedented in modern Department of Defense history. Dozens of senior uniformed officers and civilian aides were ordered to undergo polygraph examinations. The offensive reached a boiling point with the abrupt firing of Army Lieutenant General Brett Sylvia, who had been serving on the Joint Staff as the director of strategy—a linchpin role directly responsible for coordinating Pentagon planning for conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East.

The Pentagon claimed Sylvia was dismissed after failing three polygraph exams regarding leaks related to operations in Russia and Iran. Yet polygraphs remain notoriously unreliable—so much so that they are inadmissible as evidence in military courts-martial. Officials familiar with the inquiry confirmed that Sylvia categorically denied ever sharing classified or sensitive materials with the press. 

The firing sent shockwaves through the senior officer corps. Sylvia is not an isolated casualty; under Hegseth’s tenure, more than 80 generals and admirals have been removed, reassigned, or sidelined, frequently without written explanations or formal charges. Insiders describe an environment characterized by pervasive suspicion and paralyzing caution, raising questions about civilian-military relations at a moment of acute international volatility.

## The Math of Deadlock: Israel’s Unresolved Mandate

Across the Mediterranean, the aftermath of Israel’s general election has yielded another precarious stalemate. When final ballots were tabulated, neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s right-wing bloc nor the opposition coalition secured the necessary numbers to build an undisputed majority in the 120-seat Knesset.

Governing Israel requires assembling a coalition of at least 61 seats. The final tallies delivered an arithmetic knot:
* **Netanyahu Coalition:** 53 seats
* **Eisenkot Opposition Bloc:** 48 seats
* **Remaining Seats:** Distributed among independent and Arab-majority factions

The results place the country on a knife-edge. Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief of staff who campaigned on accusations that Netanyahu is steering Israel toward authoritarianism and undermining its standing abroad, faces limited pathways. He could theoretically seek common cause with Arab parties, but such a partnership remains politically fraught for conservative members within his own anti-Netanyahu coalition. Alternatively, calls for a national unity government pairing Eisenkot and Netanyahu face immense skepticism after months of vitriolic campaigning.

If political maneuvering fails to produce a coalition within statutory deadlines, Israelis could face another round of snap elections. Between 2019 and 2022, Israel went to the polls five times in an exhausting cycle of political paralysis—a cycle many voters and regional diplomats dread seeing revived.

## The Long Road Home: A 28-Year Walk Around the Earth

While governments struggled with institutional stress and electoral arithmetic, an astonishing human journey was quietly reaching its conclusion on the backroads of Western Europe.

In 1998, Karl Bushby, a former British paratrooper, stepped off from the southern tip of South America with roughly $500 in his pocket, a modified two-wheeled pushcart, and a staggering goal: to walk entirely around the planet on an unbroken foot-path, returning home to the United Kingdom without taking motorized transport.

Twenty-eight years and 31,000 miles later, Bushby’s journey is finally ending. His route took him through the Darién Gap, across the North American continent, across the frozen pack ice of the Bering Strait into Siberia, and eventually through Central Asia and Europe, culminating in a swim across the English Channel. 

"I always felt like I had something to prove," Bushby reflected during his final stretch. "And the way I was going to prove it was going out and doing something so dumb that people wouldn’t even think of it... I think that was my 'hold my beer' moment."

The trek was punctuated by geopolitics and survival crises: interrogations by Russia’s FSB, bureaucratic deportations, regional wars, and a global pandemic that forced years-long operational pauses. Each time, Bushby returned to the exact geographic coordinates where his journey had been halted.

The toll, however, was intensely personal. When Bushby began his odyssey, his son was an eight-year-old boy. Today, his son is 36, and Bushby is a grandfather to a child he has never held. Now slated to arrive at his mother’s home in Hull, England, the 55-year-old traveler admitted that the end of his life's defining endeavor brings mixed emotions.

"My purpose for living is about to abruptly end," Bushby said. "It’s been half my life... It’s going to be a big day when I arrive. And I’m not sure how I’m going to feel the next morning."

In Ohio alone, where incumbent Democrat Sherrod Brown faces a fierce challenge from Republican John Husted, ad expenditures have neared half a billion dollars.

* **Cheesecake Factory Diplomacy:** Former President Barack Obama turned heads on the trail in Detroit when he mocked the lavish, gold-gilded renovations at the Trump White House: "He’s got the White House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory." The winner receives no cash prize—only the privilege of welcoming millions of arrivals with a hearty, "We’re wicked glad you’re here."

* **Remembering Patrick Stewart:** The performing arts community is mourning the loss of Sir Patrick Stewart, who passed away at the age of 86. Born in Yorkshire, England, Stewart painstakingly softened his native Northern dialect to rise through the Royal Shakespeare Company before reinventing science fiction television. As Captain Jean-Luc Picard on *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, Stewart famously rejected the swashbuckling space-cowboy archetypes of earlier eras, crafting a commander who preferred Earl Grey tea, diplomatic patience, and classical literature—forever transforming the standard for intellectual heroes on screen.

# The Anatomy of the “Trump Slump”: Inside the Working-Class Backlash on the Las Vegas Strip

Just a few miles east of the neon glow of the Las Vegas Strip, tucked into an unassuming union hall on Commerce Street, the real machinery of Nevada’s economy gathers on folding chairs. These are not the high-rollers or casino executives who grace the promotional brochures. They are guest room attendants, banquet cooks, linen porters, and dishwashers—the predominantly immigrant and Latino workforce whose labor underwrites the world’s entertainment capital.

On a crisp Saturday marking the kickoff of early voting, hundreds of members of the Culinary Workers Union Local 226 squeezed shoulder-to-shoulder inside the headquarters. The mood was electric, fueled by coffee and chants in English and Spanish: *“When we vote, we win!”* But beneath the well-drilled political rallies lies an acute anxiety. 

In 2024, Donald Trump carried Nevada, leveraging working-class frustration over the soaring cost of living and post-pandemic inflation. Today, that coalition is showing deep fissures. On the ground in Clark County, where election margins are won or lost in the margins of individual precincts, union organizers describe an emerging and bitter phenomenon: the “Trump slump.”

Across Las Vegas, working-class voters are navigating the collision between sweeping federal policy and the fragile ecosystem of a service-based economy. Slashed work hours, aggressive immigration crackdowns, and retaliatory trade disputes with foreign neighbors have begun taking an undeniable toll on tourism. Now, the workers who keep the Strip running are hitting the pavement to answer a pivotal question: What happens when the voters who gave Trump a second chance decide the fine print cost them too much?

---

## The Ground Game in the Desert

The Culinary Union is widely regarded as one of the most formidable political operations in the United States. Representing approximately 60,000 workers across Las Vegas and Reno, Local 226 has perfected the art of ground-game mobilization over decades of contract battles and get-out-the-vote campaigns. 

Unlike conventional political campaigns that rely heavily on television airtime and digital ads, the Culinary Union’s weapon of choice is face-to-face contact. In the weeks leading up to an election, hundreds of workers take unpaid leaves of absence from their hotel and casino jobs to become full-time canvassers. Working ten-hour shifts from morning to night, six days a week, they march through the sprawling subdivisions of North Las Vegas and East Vegas, knocking on tens of thousands of doors.

For these workers, organizing is personal, born from lived experience. At the canvas launch, long-time member Irene Rodriguez stood before her peers to tell her story. She moved to the desert in 1992, defying her husband’s complaints about the sweltering heat, after calculating that a union job in Las Vegas could provide her family with a middle-class foundation. Thirty-three years later, she remains on the job, convinced that collective bargaining and political mobilization saved her family from economic precarity.

“Workers listen to workers,” says Diana Valles, who took over as president of the Culinary Union in 2022 after spending more than three decades as a rank-and-file guest room attendant. “When you knock on a door, we’re not really talking about politics in the abstract. We’re talking about the reality on the ground. We ask: Are you happy with the cost of living? How do you feel every time you fill up your gas tank, or when you buy groceries? How do you feel about healthcare costs? That’s where the conversation starts.”

Yet running this playbook has grown increasingly complex. Just because union leadership endorses Democratic candidates does not mean the rank and file automatically vote in lockstep. In 2024, substantial numbers of service workers—fatigued by years of post-COVID inflation and seeking economic disruption—cast their ballots for Donald Trump.

---

## When the Tourists Stay Home

Today, that disruption has arrived, but not in the way many service workers envisioned.

Las Vegas lives and dies by foot traffic. When travelers stay away, the consequences cascade through the supply chain with brutal efficiency. According to union officials and hospitality staff, the administration’s aggressive tariff threats against Mexico and Canada, paired with heated rhetoric toward international neighbors, delivered an immediate blow to hotel bookings. 

“Canadian tourism is down roughly 30 percent,” Valles points out. “Canadians are the tourists who stay longer and spend significant money in Las Vegas, alongside visitors from Mexico. When the president publicly trashes citizens of our closest neighbors and threatens tariffs, people simply take their vacations elsewhere.”

The dip is not confined to foreign travel. Organizers note that working-class Latinos from Southern California, who historically drove in on weekends to fill hotel rooms and restaurant tables, are increasingly staying home. The visibility of heightened federal immigration operations and street-level detentions has created a chilling effect that extends well beyond undocumented populations, rippling through mixed-status families and immigrant communities across the American Southwest.

The fallout on the casino floor is direct. Restaurants inside major resorts have shortened their operating hours or shuttered entirely. Room cleaners who once relied on steady 40-hour weeks now find their schedules cut down to four-hour shifts across three days.

“I’m a linen attendant,” explains Al Tate, a hotel worker who has canvassed for the union since 2018. “If there are no tourists, we don’t sell rooms. If we don’t sell rooms, guest room attendants get laid off. Then linen attendants get laid off, and housemen get laid off. As soon as we thought we were coming out of the pandemic, we hit the tariffs and the crackdowns, and it put us right back in that same squeeze.”

Tate summarizes the grassroots mood with quiet candor: “Whenever people’s money goes down, their attitude goes up. It’s a lot of buyer’s remorse. People at work are saying, ‘This isn’t what I signed up for.’ But if you read the fine print, this is exactly what was coming.”

---

## The Immigration Disconnect

Beyond pocketbook issues, the union’s base is grappling with the acute human costs of the administration's immigration policy. The Culinary Union’s membership is overwhelmingly Latino and heavily composed of immigrants, many of whom have lived and worked in the city for decades.

During the 2024 campaign, conservative messaging argued that proposed deportations would focus strictly on violent offenders. For some working-class Latino voters who supported Trump, that distinction was crucial. But organizers on the ground say the reality has proven far more indiscriminate.

“He said they were going to deport violent criminals, but that isn’t what people are seeing,” Valles says. “They see working fathers and mothers being detained. Families are being separated. Ten years ago, if someone had told me this would happen in this country, I wouldn’t have believed it.”

This divergence between campaign rhetoric and enforcement reality has made immigration a flashpoint on the doorsteps of Nevada’s residential neighborhoods. Rather than an abstract debate played out on cable news panels, immigration policy is being felt in the quiet absence of neighbors, empty seats in school classrooms, and workers afraid to commute to the Strip.

---

## The Porch-Step Strategy

On the streets of Northeast Las Vegas, canvassers like Jessica Palomino do not lead with partisan rhetoric. Palomino, who has spent eight years working in the culinary sector, knows that voters are tired of standard political talking points. 

Instead, she listens.

When encountering voters who are alienated by both major political parties—or conservative-leaning voters disillusioned by current conditions but skeptical of Democrats—the pitch centers entirely on domestic survival.

“People are telling us the food pantries are running low, rents are through the roof, and nobody can afford to buy a home,” Palomino says. “When someone tells me they don’t like politics, or that they disagree with certain Democratic social stances, I ask them about their families: ‘Do you have children?’ Nevada ranks 49th in education. If you don’t want to make a change for yourself, make it for your kids. If you’re working two jobs just to put food on the table, vote for their future.”

The approach proves its mettle during a canvass run when organizers encounter Adriana, a local resident who recently moved to the area. Leaning against her front doorway, Adriana admits she never pays attention to politics and had no intention of voting, believing her voice carried no weight.

Canvassers engage her not on party platforms, but on neighborhood safety, funding for public schools, and the importance of civic participation for working mothers. Within minutes, the dynamic shifts. Adriana nods, asks where the closest polling site is located, and confirms she will head to the nearby recreation center to take advantage of same-day voter registration.

“That’s why we do this,” Tate says after walking down the driveway. “Someone who had zero intention of participating walked away with a plan to vote. You can’t get that through a television screen.”

---

## Cracks in the Coalition: Class vs. Culture

The political battleground in Nevada also highlights deeper tensions running through the American electorate, particularly the durability of multiracial working-class coalitions. In recent election cycles, conservative strategists found success driving wedges between demographic groups—specifically attempting to pit working-class Black and Latino voters against one another through debates over immigration and job competition.

Inside the union, however, leaders maintain that shared workplace realities provide a counterweight to cultural division. 

“You hear that talk sometimes in the community: ‘Hispanics are taking the jobs,’” Tate, who is Black, reflects. “But then you look at the job site. We’re working side by side. We know those narratives are lies meant to pull us apart. The Black and brown communities on the job are tight. We share the same struggles, pay the same high prices, and want the same opportunities.”

Palomino agrees, arguing that political cynicism thrives in isolation. “The problem today is that people don’t talk face-to-face. Everything is on the internet. When you get out into the neighborhoods and speak to real people, you realize whether you’re Black, brown, or white, we share the same pain and the same dreams. Lower costs and fair wages help everybody.”

---

## The Broader Horizon

The stakes in Nevada extend far beyond the current midterm cycle. Recognizing the state’s demographic dynamism and its critical labor presence, the Democratic National Committee recently elevated Nevada to the second slot in its official presidential primary calendar. 

That shift transforms the Culinary Union into a pivotal national gatekeeper for 2028 and beyond. Prospective presidential candidates, including Vice President Kamala Harris, have made frequent pilgrimages to the union’s hall, seeking the blessing of an organization capable of turning out tens of thousands of reliable votes.

Yet Diana Valles insists the union’s loyalty cannot be taken for granted by any political party. 

“Our union is not an arm of any party,” Valles says. “We support the people who support our issues. Issues with the boss will always be there, and issues with politicians will always be there. That’s why our ultimate goal isn’t just endorsing candidates—it’s running our own members for elected office, so working people have a seat at the table.”

As Nevada tests the national temperature, the view from the Las Vegas pavement offers a sharp counterpoint to Beltway commentary. Voters here are weary of standard ideological boxes. What the “Trump slump” demonstrates is not necessarily a sudden ideological conversion to the Democratic platform, but a sharp, practical reaction to economic instability and policy overreach. 

For the hospitality workers knocking on doors under the desert sun, politics is stripped of its abstract theories. It comes down to shifts on the schedule, the price of groceries, and the fundamental promise that a hard day’s work in the shadow of the world's richest casinos ought to be enough to build a life.


알래스카에서는 이른바 **'투표용지 얹기(Ballot Stacking)'** 정치 공작이 수사 대상에 올랐습니다. 민주당 메리 펠톨라(Mary Peltola) 상원의원 후보 측이 공화당 현역 의원인 댄 설리번(Dan Sullivan)과 동명이인인 인물을 후보로 출마시켜 표를 분산시키려 했다는 뉴욕타임스(NYT)의 보도가 나온 직후, 법무부는 관계자들에게 무더기 소환장을 발부했습니다.

유권자에게 혼란을 주는 이 같은 교란 작전은 사실 양당이 수십 년간 암묵적으로 사용해 온 고전적 수법입니다. 실제로 올해 공화당 역시 알래스카 예비선거에서 민주당 표를 분산시키기 위해 유사한 전략을 사용한 바 있습니다. 도덕적 비판과 별개로 법적으로는 합법에 가깝다는 것이 중론입니다. 그럼에도 트럼프 대통령의 지시에 따라 법무부는 공화당 후보가 미세한 차이로 패할 경우 선거 결과 승인을 막기 위한 소송까지 제기했으나, 법률 전문가들은 이 소송의 승소 가능성을 매우 낮게 보고 있습니다.

## 펜타곤의 '불안과 파라노이아': 피트 헥세스와 80여 장성의 잔혹사

미 국방부(펜타곤) 내부 분위기는 그 어느 때보다 가라앉아 있습니다. 이란과의 전쟁으로 인해 미국의 무기 재고가 심각하게 고갈되었다는 언론 보도가 나온 이후, 피트 헥세스(Pete Hegseth) 국방장관은 기밀 유출자와의 전쟁을 선포했습니다.

> "기밀 정보에 접근할 수 있다는 것은 거룩한 신뢰입니다. 그 신뢰를 배신하는 자는 법의 엄중한 심판을 받게 될 것입니다. 기밀 유출은 장병들의 목숨을 위협합니다."  
> — *피트 헥세스 미 국방장관*

국방부 조사관들은 장성급 고위 인사를 포함한 수십 명의 군 관계자들에게 거짓말탐지기(Polygraph) 조사를 실시했습니다. 이는 현대 미국 역사상 전례가 없는 강경 조치입니다.

```
[펜타곤 기밀 유출 조사 및 인사 파동]
- 조사 방식: 고위 장성 대상 거짓말탐지기(Polygraph) 강제 실시
- 핵심 경출 사례: 브렛 실비아(Brett Sylvia) 육군 중장 해임
  └─ 사유: 거짓말탐지기 3회 탈락 (본인은 혐의 강력 부인)
  └─ 법적 문제: 거짓말탐지기는 군사법원에서 증거 능력 미인정
- 조직 내부 여파: 장성·제독 80여 명 해임 및 보직 해임 → 심각한 불신과 파라노이아 형성
```

이 과정에서 합동참모본부 전략방침국장으로서 우크라이나, 이란 등 전 세계 군사 작전 수립의 핵심 역할을 맡아온 브렛 실비아(Brett Sylvia) 육군 중장이 전격 해임되었습니다. 국방부는 그가 거짓말탐지기 조사에서 세 차례 탈락했다는 이유 외에 어떠한 물증도 제시하지 못했습니다. 거짓말탐지기는 신뢰성이 떨어져 미 군사법원에서도 증거로 채택되지 않는 장치입니다. 실비아 중장은 혐의를 강력히 부인했으나 수용되지 않았고, 군 고위 간부들은 깊은 충격과 분노를 표출하고 있습니다.

헥세스 장관 취임 이후 해임되거나 보직에서 해임된 장성과 제독은 80명이 넘습니다. 대다수는 자신이 왜 쫓겨나는지 명확한 이유조차 듣지 못했습니다. 펜타곤 내부자들은 현재 국방부가 '지독한 불신과 파라노이아(기형적 의심)'에 휩싸여 있다고 전합니다.

# 라스베이거스의 화려한 불빛 뒤편, 요동치는 '민심의 바로미터': 네바다 노동자들이 말하는 ‘트럼프 슬럼프’와 미국의 선택

## 카지노의 심장을 뛰게 하는 사람들: ‘조리 노동자 연합(Culinary Union)’

라스베이거스와 리노 지역을 아우르는 조리 노동자 연합(Culinary Workers Union)은 약 6만 명의 환대 산업(Hospitality Industry) 종사자를 대표하는 막강한 조직이다. 이들의 대다수는 히스패닉과 흑인 등 유색인종이자 이민자 출신의 노동자들이다. 

33년 전 라스베이거스에 정착해 룸 어텐던트로 일해온 아이린 로드리게스(Irene Rodriguez) 같은 이들이 이 조직의 척추를 형성하고 있다. 이 조합은 단순한 노동단체를 넘어, 네바다주는 물론 미국 대선 판세까지 좌우하는 핵심 정치 세력으로 기능해 왔다. 카멀라 해리스 부통령을 비롯한 주요 정치인들이 선거철마다 이곳 본부를 찾는 이유도 바로 이 때문이다.

그러나 지도부의 전통적인 민주당 지지 성향과 달리, 2024년 대선 당시 조합원 상당수는 도널드 트럼프에게 표를 던졌다. 기성 정치권에 대한 실망과 경제적 변화에 대한 갈망이 작용한 결과였다. 하지만 불과 1~2년 만에 이들의 기대는 깊은 회의감으로 바뀌고 있다.

## 덮쳐오는 위기, ‘트럼프 슬럼프(Trump Slump)’의 실체

2022년부터 노조를 이끌고 있는 다이애나 바예스(Diana Valles) 위원장은 현장의 위기를 ‘트럼프 슬럼프(Trump Slump)’라는 한 단어로 압축했다. 트럼프 행정부의 정책 기조가 네바다 경제의 생명줄인 관광 산업에 직격탄을 날렸다는 진단이다.

### 1. 관광객 급감과 노동 시간 축소
캐나다와 멕시코 등 주요 인접국을 겨냥한 관세 인상과 적대적 수사는 즉각적인 부메랑이 되어 돌아왔다. 
* 라스베이거스를 찾아 장기 체류하며 소비를 주도하던 **캐나다인 관광객은 30%나 급감**했다.
* 주말마다 네바다를 찾던 캘리포니아의 히스패닉계 방문객들 역시 무차별적인 이민 단속 분위기 속에서 발길을 끊었다.
* 관광객 감소는 노동자들의 생계 파탄으로 직결되었다. 통상 하루 8시간, 주 5일 일하던 노동자들의 교대 근무(Shift)는 하루 4시간, 주 3일로 반토막 났다. 안정적인 일자리를 자랑하던 호텔 카지노 노동자들조차 렌트비와 생활비를 감당하지 못해 허덕이는 처지가 된 것이다.

### 2. ‘표적 단속’ 약속의 배신과 공포
더 심각한 것은 지역 사회를 짓누르는 공포다. 트럼프 전 대통령은 선거 기간 동안 ‘폭력 범죄자 추방’을 공언했으나, 실제 현장에서 벌어진 일은 평범한 노동자 계층을 향한 무차별적 체포와 억류였다. 

바예스 위원장은 "사람들은 거리에서 평범한 일하는 부모들이 자녀와 격리되고 끌려가는 현실에 표를 준 것이 아니다"라며, "10년 전만 해도 이 나라에서 이런 일이 일어나리라곤 상상조차 할 수 없었다"고 토로했다. 미국 시민권을 가진 유색인종마저 위축시키는 강경책은 노동자들의 안전과 지역 상권을 동시에 마비시키고 있다.

## 문을 두드리는 발걸음: "정치 구호 대신 삶의 이야기를 나눈다"

조기 투표(Early Voting)가 시작된 주말, 수백 명의 노조 조합원들이 본부 앞에 모여들었다. 이들은 선거 기간 동안 생업을 잠시 내려놓고 무보수 혹은 노조 지원 휴직계를 낸 채 가가호호 방문(Canvassing, 호별 방문 선거운동)에 나선다.

호텔 린넨 담당자로 일하며 2018년부터 유권자 접촉 활동을 해온 앨런 테이트(Allen Tate)는 현장의 분위기가 그 어느 때보다 무겁다고 전한다.
> "사람들의 주머니 사정이 나빠지면, 태도는 날카로워집니다. 호텔에 투숙객이 없으면 방이 안 팔리고, 룸 어텐던트와 린넨 담당자 모두 줄줄이 해고됩니다. 사람들은 일하러 가기도, 네바다를 방문하기도 무서워합니다. '내가 이런 꼴을 보려고 표를 준 게 아닌데'라는 후회가 곳곳에서 터져 나옵니다."

8년 차 카지노 노동자인 제시카 팔로미노(Jessica Palomino) 역시 집집마다 문을 두드리며 마주하는 주민들의 절박함을 전했다. 무료 급식소(Food Pantry)의 음식은 바닥나고, 치솟는 임대료에 주택 구매는 꿈도 꾸지 못하는 상황이다.

이들이 유권자를 설득하는 방식은 거창한 정당 이데올로기가 아니다.
* **생활비와 경제(Pocketbook Issues):** "식료품점에 갈 때마다, 기름을 넣을 때마다 오르는 가격에 만족하십니까?"
* **자녀의 미래:** "네바다의 공교육은 전국 49위 수준입니다. 당장 나 자신을 위해서가 아니라면, 하루 두 개의 일자리를 뛰며 살아가는 우리 아이들의 아메리칸 드림을 위해서라도 투표해야 합니다."

정치에 관심이 없다며 냉소적인 반응을 보이던 한 아이의 어머니는 자녀의 미래와 지역사회 권리에 대한 대화를 나눈 끝에 인근 레크리에이션 센터에서 당일 유권자 등록(Same-day Registration)을 마치고 투표하겠다고 마음을 바꿨다. 거대 미디어나 인터넷 토론이 결코 닿지 못하는 틈새를 사람과 사람의 대면 접촉이 메우고 있는 셈이다.

## 인위적 갈등을 넘어서는 일터의 연대

선거 국면마다 보수 정치권은 흑인과 히스패닉 커뮤니티 사이에 "이민자들이 당신의 일자리를 빼앗고 있다"는 프레임을 주입하며 표심 분할을 시도해 왔다. 2024년 대선에서도 이러한 전략은 일정 부분 유효한 듯 보였다.

그러나 현장에서 만난 노동자들의 목소리는 달랐다. 앨런과 제시카는 이러한 분열 조장이 현장의 현실과는 동떨어진 '거짓말'이라고 단언했다.
> "우리가 일하는 현장에서 흑인과 갈색(라틴계) 노동자들은 늘 하나로 뭉쳐 있습니다. 우리는 같은 고통을 겪고, 같은 꿈을 꿉니다. 피부색이 검든, 갈색이든 중요하지 않습니다. 사회가 파편화되고 온라인에 갇힐수록 오해가 쌓이지만, 직접 얼굴을 마주보고 삶의 문제를 이야기하면 누구나 같은 인간이라는 사실을 깨닫게 됩니다."

## 좌우의 대립을 넘어 ‘위와 아래’의 대결로

네바다 민심의 흐름은 이번 중간선거를 넘어 다가오는 2028년 미국 대선 판도의 중대한 시험대다. 민주당은 최근 네바다주를 대선 후보 경선의 두 번째 순서로 전진 배치하며 이 지역 노동자 계층의 상징성을 공식화했다. 조리 노동자 연합은 단순한 표밭을 넘어, 자신들의 이익을 대변할 수 있는 노동자 출신 후보를 직접 공직에 출마시키는 방식으로 권력 지형을 바꾸려 하고 있다.

네바다 현장에서 확인된 민심은 미국 정치가 단순히 '좌파 대 우파(Left vs. Right)'의 구도로만 설명될 수 없음을 웅변한다. 지금 유권자들이 느끼는 갈등의 본질은 평범한 노동자들의 삶과 유리된 채 작동하는 권력, 즉 **‘밑바닥 대 꼭대기(Bottom vs. Top)’의 대결**에 가깝다.

트럼프 전 대통령을 둘러싼 논란의 핵심 역시 그의 개인적 자질을 넘어, 그가 내놓은 고율 관세와 극단적 이민 배척 정책이 다수 노동 계층의 실질적인 삶과 완전히 엇박자를 내고 있다는 점에 있다. 화려한 카지노 도시 라스베이거스의 뒷골목에서 문을 두드리는 노동자들의 발걸음은, 미국의 정치가 진정으로 귀 기울여야 할 대상이 누구인지를 명확하게 가리키고 있다.
