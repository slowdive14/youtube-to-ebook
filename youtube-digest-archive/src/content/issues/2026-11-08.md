# The Genius of Staying True: Sterlin Harjo on Pirated Cable, Popes, and the Lowdown Hoedown

At the historic Studebaker Theater in downtown Chicago, the audience of NPR’s *Wait Wait... Don’t Tell Me!* gathered for a signature blend of sharp political satire, absurd news trivia, and cultural commentary. Among the laughter, panel banter, and game shows sat a guest whose rise in American television represents one of the most refreshing cultural shifts in recent memory: Sterlin Harjo.

Harjo—the visionary filmmaker, Seminole/Muscogee Creek storyteller, and creator of the critically acclaimed series *Reservation Dogs* and *The Lowdown*—is one of the rare television producers to be awarded a prestigious MacArthur "Genius" Grant. Yet, as he settled onto the stage alongside host Peter Sagal, guest host Alonzo Slade, and panelists Brian Babylon, Adam Felber, and Joyelle Nicole Johnson, Harjo revealed that his path to genius was paved less by traditional prestige and more by pirated cable, uncollected student loans, and a healthy dose of small-town Oklahoma mischief.

---

## From "Harjo’s Martial Arts" to Sundance Acclaim

Growing up in rural Oklahoma, Harjo never envisioned a career in Hollywood, largely because the media landscape of his youth offered virtually no accurate reflection of Native American life. 

"It’s so far removed from who I am and the people that I grew up with," Harjo recalled, describing old Westerns he watched as a child. He recounted a specific memory of his father calling him into the living room to watch a movie titled *Seminole Wars*. Expecting to see his own heritage represented, Harjo was greeted by non-Native actors dressed in Cheyenne regalia, speaking Lakota. "We had a good laugh at that, but then also just the idea of being represented was like, *wow*. I didn’t have a *Reservation Dogs* growing up."

Without a local blueprint for filmmaking, Harjo’s early years were occupied by other pursuits—namely, his father’s unique approach to martial arts. His father ran "Harjo’s Martial Arts," a studio that fused traditional Taekwondo with a Bruce Lee-inspired, DIY discipline. Harjo excelled, eventually ranking high in the state as a brown belt before secondary school distractions took over.

"I think I discovered girls and football," Harjo joked, "and I was like, 'I have no time for this, Dad.'"

When the filmmaking itch finally bit, it was powered by resourcefulness. A family friend who worked for the local cable company hooked up a descrambler box for free, allowing young Harjo to devour cinema endlessly. Later, while attending college, Harjo took out a student loan, bought a camera and a computer, and taught himself how to edit footage. 

He never paid the loan back. 

"They were after me well into after I went to Sundance and had films in festivals," Harjo laughed. "They were still calling me and trying to sue me. I just kind of felt like the universe owed that to me."

Even his MacArthur "Genius" Grant victory came with a classic Harjo twist. When the MacArthur Foundation tried to reach him with news of the $800,000 prize, Harjo dodged their calls for two full weeks. Assuming the foundation was simply trying to book him for an unpaid panel discussion, he instructed his assistant to ignore them until she finally insisted he take the call.

That grounded, unapologetically local perspective is precisely why Harjo continues to live and shoot his projects in Oklahoma rather than relocating to Los Angeles or New York. When asked about other productions set in the state—such as Sylvester Stallone’s *Tulsa King*, which relocated to Atlanta after shooting its pilot in Oklahoma—Harjo wore his local pride like a badge of honor. He revealed that his production company even printed t-shirts featuring a famously colorful quote from Stallone: *"Shooting in Oklahoma prepared me for a lifetime in hell."*

## The Absurdity of the Modern News Cycle

Harjo’s appearance anchored an episode packed with the bizarre, hilarious realities of contemporary culture. As the panel navigated the week's news quiz, several absurd trends came to light.

### Aging Millennials and the Denial of Sight
A recent feature in *The Wall Street Journal* highlighted a growing cultural phenomenon: initial-generation millennials (now turning 40) entering deep denial about their declining eyesight. Rather than buying reading glasses, forty-somethings are resorting to elaborate workarounds, such as taking photos of restaurant menus on their smartphones and zooming in, or relying heavily on high-intensity flashlight apps. 

As the panel noted, while red-light masks, obsessively curated skincare, and high-intensity interval training can preserve a youthful facade, nothing breaks the spell quite like holding a menu at arm's length while squinting in a dimly lit bistro.

### Popes, Druids, and Cinematic Hypnosis
The news roundup also touched on a surreal Vatican meeting between Pope Leo and legendary singer-songwriter Bob Dylan. Photos of the encounter sparked online amusement: while the Pope wore his traditional white cassock, Dylan arrived draped in a black hoodie, looking less like a folk icon and more like an octogenarian druid.

Meanwhile, in the world of cinema, theaters worldwide are resorting to unprecedented measures to lure audiences back to the big screen. The winning story of the week came from the Netherlands, where a Dutch theater announced plans to hire a certified hypnotist with 30 years of experience. The goal? To mass-hypnotize moviegoers into temporarily forgetting plot twists from iconic films like *Seven* or *The Matrix*, allowing them to experience the shock and awe of the narrative for the very first time.

### Bone Marrow Cakes and Corporate Small Talk
Outside of entertainment, the panel tackled two of the stranger lifestyle developments of late:
* **The "No-Sugar" Toddler Birthday Cake:** A popular mom influencer made headlines after bragging about avoiding refined sugar for her toddler's birthday. Instead of a traditional baked treat, she carved a watermelon into the shape of a cake and frosted it entirely with whipped bone marrow—a savory creation panelists likened to something served to a zoo animal on its birthday.
* **The Chief Small Talk Officer:** In an effort to coax remote workers back into physical spaces and revitalize office camaraderie, tech firm Rise Guide posted a job opening for a "Chief Small Talk Officer." The role is designed to foster spontaneous, positive conversations around the office water cooler, formalizing the art of casual chitchat.

## Lowdown Meets Hoedown: Testing the Genius

To cap off his appearance, Harjo was subjected to the show's signature game, *Not My Job*, where accomplished guests are quizzed on subjects far outside their expertise. In honor of his hit show *The Lowdown*, Harjo was quizzed on the history and terminology of the American **Hoedown**—specifically, square dancing.

Despite his initial skepticism, Harjo tapped into his Oklahoma roots, correctly identifying historical trivia that left the crowd both entertained and educated:

1. **The Anti-Jazz Campaign:** Harjo learned that the proliferation of square dancing in American public school physical education programs during the mid-20th century was largely bankrolled and promoted by industrialist Henry Ford. Ford believed that traditional folk dancing would counter what he viewed as the "corrupting" influence of jazz music on American youth.
2. **Tractor Square Dancing:** Harjo correctly guessed the existence of tractor square dancing—a real Midwestern tradition, showcased annually in towns like Neaha, Iowa, where skilled drivers maneuver heavy farm machinery in synchronized, geometric square dance patterns.
3. **Advanced Calls:** When quizzed on real square dancing commands, Harjo discovered that calls such as *"Slip the clutch,"* *"Peel the top,"* and *"Shoot through the hole in the old tin can"* are all authentic terminology used by professional callers.

By sweeping the quiz, Harjo earned a custom voicemail recording for a lucky listener contestant, officially securing his status as an NPR-approved "genius."

## Slippers, Mosh Pits, and Unfiltered Authenticity

The episode closed with a look at household safety and generational shifts. Emergency room data recently revealed that indoor slippers are statistically the single most dangerous shoe style in terms of annual injuries, causing over 20,000 hospital visits a year due to trips, slips, and a lack of traction. 

Concurrently, the panel reflected on how the line between youth culture and parenthood has blurred. From parents joining their adult children in metal mosh pits at Metallica and Green Day concerts to panelist Joyelle Nicole Johnson recalling how she and her mother used to crash high-profile celebrity funerals together—including those of Rick James and Ossie Davis—the modern landscape is defined by breaking old rules.

In many ways, Sterlin Harjo’s career reflects that exact spirit. By rejecting traditional Hollywood formulas, staying grounded in his community, and trusting that hyper-local stories carry universal human weight, Harjo has proven that genius isn't about fitting into an established mold. It's about having the courage to tell your own story—even if you had to pirate the cable box to learn how.

# The Conductor’s Counteroffensive: Chuck Schumer’s Blueprint for Hemming In Trump

In the quiet corridors of the United States Capitol, an office fireplace that had sat cold for hours suddenly roared to life, casting an amber glow across Chuck Schumer’s desk. Hanging nearby was a framed Norman Rockwell painting of Lyndon B. Johnson, and beside it, an artifact of raw political trauma: the framed tabulation of the 2020 electoral votes certified on January 6, 2021.

Looking at the certification sheet, the veteran New Yorker recalled the harrowing moments when a police officer shielded him from rioters who had breached the building, shouting anti-Semitic threats just thirty feet away. “Still here,” Schumer remarked.

That phrase could just as easily describe his political survival. 

Barely eighteen months earlier, the Democratic Party was stranded in a barren political wilderness. Having lost the White House and the Senate in sweeping fashion, Democratic activists were actively demanding Schumer’s head, castigating him as an aging institutionalist whose tactical caution had failed to curb Donald Trump’s aggressive second-term agenda. Pundits openly questioned whether the Senate minority leader would even finish his term.

Yet following a thunderous midterm election, the landscape has radically inverted. Democrats did not merely claw back the Senate—they swept to a commanding 54-seat majority that could widen to 56 once outstanding ballots in Alaska and Mississippi are finalized. Across the rotunda, the Democratic House majority surged past 250 seats, its largest margin in nearly two decades. Schumer is once again preparing to hold the gavel as Senate Majority Leader.

Now comes the harder part: governing a fractious, populist Washington opposite a combative president whom Schumer bluntly characterizes as "the most corrupt leader America has ever had."

---

## The Three C’s and the Guilt-Trip Strategy

Schumer attributes the Democratic resurgence to an unyielding, almost monomaniacal message discipline centered on three words: **Cost, Chaos, and Corruption.**

“When in January of 2025, right after we lost the presidency and the Senate, I said, ‘We’re going to take it back in 2026,’ everyone rolled their eyes,” Schumer recounted. The turnaround, he insists, was achieved not by indulging the party’s internal ideological squabbles, but by anchoring every campaign message to pocketbook survival. 

Where the party previously found itself trapped in reactionary skirmishes over culture-war talking points—from transgender healthcare debates to immigration semantics—Schumer ordered candidates to bypass conservative traps entirely. Instead, Democrats hammered Trump’s second-term economic shocks: sweeping cross-border tariffs that disrupted Midwestern agricultural markets, the gutting of Affordable Care Act subsidies, soaring grocery prices, and high electric bills driven by unbridled data-center expansion.

```
THE DEMOCRATIC MIDTERM RECOVERY: CORE PILLARS
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. COSTS: Focus relentlessly on daily expenses              │
│    • Grocery inflation, energy bills, prescription drugs    │
│    • Protection of Affordable Care Act subsidies            │
│                                                             │
│ 2. CORRUPTION: Weaponize executive overreach                │
│    • Scrutiny of the administration's controversial fund    │
│    • Oversight demands, cabinet accountability              │
│                                                             │
│ 3. CHAOS: Frame White House policy as volatile disruption   │
│    • Foreign entanglements and unauthorized war funding     │
│    • Cross-border tariffs hitting manufacturing & farming   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The second pillar of the strategy was aggressively expanding the map by courting candidates who reflected their states rather than national primary orthodoxy. That meant personal, unrelenting recruiting visits by Schumer himself.

To reclaim Ohio, Schumer flew to Columbus three separate times to coax retired Senator Sherrod Brown back into the arena. On his third visit, sitting with Brown and his wife, the Pulitzer Prize-winning columnist Connie Schultz, Schumer deployed unabashed peer pressure:

> *"Sherrod, Connie, look, you can have a great retirement. You’ve had a terrific career. But if you don’t run, and you’re the only one who can win in Ohio, and we lose the Senate by one vote, you’re not going to enjoy your retirement that much."*

The following day, Brown called back to say he was in.

In Mississippi, Schumer invited civil rights attorney Scott Colom—whose federal judicial nomination had previously been blocked by Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith—to his personal apartment in Brooklyn for lunch, urging him to launch a bid that has placed the seat within razor-thin striking distance. 

Even where Schumer’s initial establishment picks stumbled in primaries—such as in Michigan, where progressive Abdul El-Sayed defeated Haley Stevens—the Democratic machine pivoted rapidly, pouring tens of millions of dollars into coalition-building that ultimately crossed the finish line.

---

## Referendum or Rebirth?

Despite the celebratory mood among congressional Democrats, an uncomfortable reality underpins their victory: voters remain deeply skeptical of the party itself. In the final stretch before the midterm election, national polling showed that barely 28 percent of voters held a favorable view of the Democratic Party. By several metrics, Democrats were polling below the very president they sought to repudiate.

Was this election an affirmative endorsement of Democratic vision, or merely a frantic public grasping for any emergency brake against a turbulent administration?

Schumer concedes it was an undeniable mixture of both. But he argues that Senate Republicans committed political malpractice by refusing to break from the White House, even as tariffs cratered auto manufacturing in Michigan and soybean farming across the Plains.

“Almost everything [Trump] did was either irrelevant to the American people’s main concern, costs, or made it worse,” Schumer argued. “The Senate Republicans, who are savvy people, they just followed Trump over the cliff. I always thought the way they could deter us was to break from Trump. And they didn’t.”

By remaining tethered to the president’s controversial moves—including a foreign military conflict abroad and a contentious multibillion-dollar presidential defamation settlement that critics have labeled an executive slush fund—vulnerable Republicans surrendered their local identities to national discontent.

---

## Governing Over a Presidential Veto

With majorities in both chambers, the immediate question facing Schumer and incoming House leaders is how to turn legislative control into concrete policy when the man in the Oval Office commands a veto pen.

Schumer’s answer is straightforward: treat the White House as structurally secondary.

“We are going to push our agenda, what we believe in, with him or without him,” Schumer insisted. “He’s, in a certain sense, irrelevant to us. If he joins us—I doubt he will—great. If he doesn’t, we’re still moving forward.”

The emerging Democratic playbook involves sending targeted, hyper-popular bills to the president's desk, daring him to strike them down:
* **Targeting Tariffs:** Passing bipartisan legislation to revoke tariffs that have spiked consumer goods and retaliatory agricultural duties.
* **Prescription & Health Costs:** Reauthorizing ACA premium subsidies and mandating caps on essential pharmaceuticals.
* **Housing and Child Care:** Advancing federal zoning incentives and direct construction subsidies to alleviate urban housing deficits.
* **Power of the Purse:** Invoking constitutional spending limits to choke off unauthorized appropriations for foreign military interventions.

Schumer calculates that this dynamic creates an asymmetric win-win. If moderate and battleground Republicans cross the aisle to break filibusters and stave off local outrage, life becomes demonstrably more affordable for everyday Americans. If Trump vetoes the packages, Democrats obtain pristine contrast ads heading straight into the 2028 presidential campaign.

“If Trump blocks everything, and we are clear, united, and relentless, 2028 gives us the trifecta,” Schumer noted coolly. “The night of the 2026 elections will look like a good night to them compared to what will happen in 2028.”

---

## Generational Tension and the Filibuster Question

Yet the path forward is laden with structural pitfalls inside Schumer’s own ranks.

The incoming Democratic freshman class includes combative, reform-minded progressives like Texas’s James Talarico, who have made no secret of their appetite to scrap the 60-vote legislative filibuster. They argue that maintaining a supermajority requirement renders the new majority toothless, preventing the party from passing foundational measures on voting access, labor rights, and court reform.

When pressed on whether he would entertain abolishing the filibuster to enact his agenda, Schumer remained deliberately noncommittal. “Our focus has to be on reducing costs,” he deflected, repeating that every procedural choice will be viewed “through the prism of succeeding.”

Equally delicate is the question of generational leadership. At 75, Schumer represents an aging Democratic hierarchy that has faced persistent scrutiny from an increasingly youthful base eager for succession. When asked outright whether his return as Majority Leader signals an intention to run for a sixth Senate term, Schumer neatly sidestepped:

> *"I’m focused on 2027. Delivering what we promised. Creating unity, helping these new younger members' talents shine."*

He likens his current role less to an autocrat and more to an orchestral conductor: “I make sure we stay on the same tune. But there’s a talented oboist, and they should be highlighted; there's a talented drummer, and they should be highlighted. My job is to make it one tune, but let everybody shine.”

---

## The Verdict of the 'Prism'

For two years, Chuck Schumer’s political survival depended upon unity, recruitment, and an adversarial White House overreaching in real time. That chapter has formally closed. With majorities secured across Capitol Hill, the measure of Democratic power shifts entirely from critique to output.

Schumer knows better than anyone that an electorate capable of handing Democrats sweeping majorities amid 28 percent approval ratings is an electorate with zero institutional loyalty. They are looking for immediate relief at the checkout counter, stability on the world stage, and an end to perpetual civic turbulence.

“My North Star was always winning back the Senate. Everything I did was seen through that prism,” Schumer reflected, leaning forward as the Capitol fire crackled behind him. “It worked. But now there’s a new prism: **produce**.”

* **미시시피주:** 공화당 텃밭으로 여겨지던 남부에서도 파변을 일으켰다. 공화당 신디 하이드스미스(Cindy Hyde-Smith) 의원에 의해 연방 판사 인준이 가로막혔던 흑인 법조인 스콧 콜롬(Scott Colom)을 브루클린 자택으로 초대해 점심을 대접하며 설득했다. 콜롬은 현재 1,800여 표 차로 앞서며 역사적인 승리를 눈앞에 두고 있다.
* **알래스카 및 중서부:** 보수 성향이 강한 알래스카에서는 메리 펠톨라(Mary Peltola)를 앞세웠고, 트럼프의 관세 폭탄으로 대두(콩) 수출길이 막힌 아이오와와 캔자스, 국경 갈등의 피해를 입은 메인주 등을 집중 공략했다.

슈머는 "미시간 유권자들은 자동차 산업을 뒤흔든 관세를 증오했고, 캔자스 농민들은 대두 농가를 망친 관세를 분노했다"며 "중앙당이 하나의 획일적인 메시지를 강요하지 않고, 각 지역 후보들이 자신만의 언어로 물가·부패·혼란(3C)을 이야기하도록 공간을 열어준 것이 주효했다"고 평가했다.

## 3. 원내대표의 딜레마: 세대교체 요구와 '오케스트라 지휘자론'

압도적인 승리로 의회 권력을 탈환했음에도, 척 슈머를 바라보는 시선에는 여전히 복합적인 감정이 섞여 있다. 올해로 75세, 곧 76세를 바라보는 그는 의회에서만 27년을 보낸 올드보이다. 워싱턴 정가 안팎에서는 당의 미래를 위해 젊은 세대에게 길을 터주어야 한다는 세대교체 압박이 끊이지 않는다.

그러나 차기 상원 원내대표 경선에서 그에게 도전장을 내밀 대항마는 보이지 않는다. 선거 승리로 그의 리더십이 당내에서 강력한 정당성을 확보했기 때문이다. 세대교체 요구에 대한 기자의 질문에 슈머는 자신을 **'오케스트라 지휘자'**에 비유했다.

> "내 역할은 모든 연주자가 같은 음정을 유지하도록 이끄는 지휘자입니다. 뛰어난 오보에 연주자, 재능 있는 드러머가 있다면 그들이 무대에서 가장 빛날 수 있도록 스포트라이트를 비춰주어야 합니다. 신예 의원들의 엄청난 재능을 생산적으로 발휘하게 돕는 것이 지금 내게 주어진 가장 중요한 책무입니다."

다만, 2년 뒤 본인의 상원의원 재선(6선) 도전 여부에 대해서는 "현재 내 모든 시선은 2027년의 약속을 이행하는 것에만 맞춰져 있다"며 즉답을 피했다.

## 4. 입법 청사진: 트럼프를 압박할 '5대 민생 비용'과 예산권 행사

다수당 지위를 되찾은 민주당의 당면 과제는 명확하다. 바로 트럼프 행정부에 대한 견제와 입법 성과의 도출이다. 슈머는 트럼프 대통령과의 직접적인 소통 채널에 연연하지 않겠다는 뜻을 분명히 했다. "트럼프가 동참한다면 좋겠지만, 그렇지 않더라도 우리는 직진한다. 트럼프의 동의 여부는 우리에게 부차적인 문제"라는 것이다.

슈머가 밝힌 민주당의 핵심 입법 로드맵은 다음과 같다.

### ① 5대 핵심 비용 인하 법안
민주당은 국민 체감도가 높은 다섯 가지 영역에 대한 집중 입법을 예고했다.
1. **의료비:** 오바마케어(Affordable Care Act, ACA) 보조금 복원 및 확대
2. **에너지 비용:** 전기료 및 공공요금 안정화
3. **식료품 가격:** 유통 구조 개선을 통한 장바구니 물가 억제
4. **보육 비용:** 아동 보육 지원 확대
5. **주거비:** 저렴하고 신속한 주택 공급을 위한 규제 혁신 및 연방 지원

슈머는 "트럼프의 보복 관세를 무력화하는 법안을 의회 차원에서 통과시킬 것"이라며 "과거 트럼프의 눈치를 보느라 숨죽였던 공화당 의원들도 지역구 경제가 파탄 나는 것을 확인한 만큼 상당수 표결에 동참할 것"이라고 내다봤다.

### ② 의회 예산권(Power of the Purse)을 통한 전쟁 종식
슈머는 트럼프 행정부가 벌이고 있는 대외 무력 충돌(이란 등)을 종식하기 위해 헌법이 의회에 부여한 고유 권한인 **'예산권(Power of the Purse)'**을 전면 가동하겠다고 밝혔다. 국방부가 전쟁을 지속하는 데 필요한 군사 예산을 삭감함으로써, 여론의 지지를 받지 못하는 전쟁의 동력을 물리적으로 차단하겠다는 계산이다.

### ③ 권력형 부패 척결과 특검급 조사
슈머는 트럼프 행정부를 "미국 역사상 가장 부패한 정권"으로 규정하며 강도 높은 의회 조사를 예고했다. 특히 피트 헤그세스(Pete Hegseth) 국방장관 등의 자질 논란에 대해 해임 및 탄핵을 지지한다는 입장을 숨기지 않았다. 대통령의 사적 소송 합의금 유용 의혹, 정치적 피해자 보상을 명분으로 한 비자금(Slush fund) 조성 의혹 등도 집중 타깃이다. 

다만, 그는 탄핵 추진 여부에 대해서는 하원의 몫이라며 신중론을 폈다. 부패 척결에만 매몰되어 민생(Costs) 의제를 놓칠 경우, 과거처럼 역풍을 맞을 수 있다는 경계심 때문이다.

## 5. 승리의 덫: "결과를 내지 못하면 민주당의 미래는 없다"

트럼프 대통령이 의회를 통과한 민주당 법안들에 거부권(Veto)을 행사할 경우 입법 드라이브는 교착 상태에 빠질 수 있다. 또한 상원의 입법 지연 수단인 **필리버스터(합법적 의사진행 방해, Filibuster)**를 폐지해야 한다는 당내 신예들의 요구에 대해, 슈머는 "물가 안정을 위해 가장 빠르고 최선의 경로가 무엇인지 모든 가능성을 열어두고 검토할 것"이라며 여지를 남겼다.

슈머가 그리는 정치적 시나리오는 명확하다. 법안이 트럼프의 서명을 받아 실행되면 민생을 살려 이득이고, 트럼프가 거부권을 행사해 좌초시킨다면 그 정치적 책임을 공화당에 온전히 물을 수 있다는 것이다.

> "우리가 치열하게 싸우는 모습을 국민이 본다면, 2028년 대선에서 공화당은 백악관마저 잃게 될 것입니다. 저들이 사사건건 민생 법안을 가로막는다면, 이번 2026년 중간선거의 참패는 2028년에 닥칠 파멸에 비하면 차라리 좋은 밤으로 기억될 것입니다."

그러나 인터뷰 말미, 슈머는 승리에 도취된 당을 향해 냉정한 경고를 잊지 않았다. 유권자들은 민주당이 좋아서 표를 준 것이 아니라, 변화를 이끌어낼 '기회'를 한 번 더 시험 삼아 던져준 것에 불과하다는 점을 꿰뚫고 있기 때문이다.

"일은 이제 막 시작됐을 뿐입니다. 약속을 실천해 국민의 삶을 실질적으로 나아지게 만들지 못한다면, 지금의 승리는 한순간에 신기루처럼 사라질 것입니다. 결국 마지막 심판관은 내가 아니라 유권자들입니다." 

브루클린 출신 특유의 끈질김과 실용주의로 무장한 노련한 정객 척 슈머. 트럼프 2기 후반부, 그가 이끄는 상원 다수당 민주당이 워싱턴의 혼란을 뚫고 실질적인 성과를 입증해 낼 수 있을지 전 세계의 시선이 집중되고 있다.
